CHAPTER 1: REPRODUCTION IN FLOWERING PLANTS

1.1 FLOWER - A FASCINATING ORGAN OF ANGIOSPERMS
Flowers are objects of aesthetic, ornamental, social, religious and cultural value. They are morphological and embryological marvels and the sites of sexual reproduction.

DOUBLE FERTILISATION
After entering one of the synergids, the pollen tube releases the two male gametes into the cytoplasm of the synergid. One of the male gametes moves towards the egg cell and fuses with its nucleus thus completing the syngamy. This results in the formation of a diploid cell, the zygote. The other male gamete moves towards the two polar nuclei located in the central cell and fuses with them to produce a triploid primary endosperm nucleus (PEN). As this involves the fusion of three haploid nuclei it is termed triple fusion. Since two types of fusions, syngamy and triple fusion take place in an embryo sac the phenomenon is termed double fertilisation, an event unique to angiosperms.
